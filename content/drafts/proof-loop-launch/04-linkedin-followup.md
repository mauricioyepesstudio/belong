One design decision we made with Proof Loop, our new feature in BELONG, that's easy to get wrong:

You can challenge a claim. You can never challenge a person.

When we built the Challenge and Outcome mechanics inside Proof Loop, we set a hard rule: a Challenge targets the proposition or the proposed outcome — never someone's identity, dignity, or lived experience. BELONG does not assign a Truth Score, an ideological score, or any kind of universal human-worth score, and it never will.

We also made "inconclusive" a legitimate Outcome. Not every claim resolves cleanly, and pretending otherwise produces worse incentives — people start optimizing for a clean win instead of an honest test. Changing your position after evidence shows up is treated as intellectual integrity, not a loss.

One practical example of what this makes possible: two people who genuinely disagree politically can open the same Proof, propose different Approaches, and still complete one real community project together — without BELONG deciding who was right, and without either person's stance being reframed as having "changed."

That's the behavior we're trying to build toward: disagreement that stays productive because it's anchored to a shared, testable goal instead of to each other.
